# Project Notes

## Origin

This repository captures the working discussion around converting the justice-mercy-free-will workbook and the nine-factor Master Equation into a repeatable event-analysis mechanism.

The central problem is not whether an AI can produce a persuasive moral essay. It can. The problem is whether an untrained human, a Python program, and multiple independent AI systems can receive the same evidence and produce substantially the same structural diagnosis.

## Core insight

The scoring engine must not directly score intuition. It must score fixed answers to observable questions.

```text
Internet evidence
→ atomic factual claims
→ frozen subquestions
→ yes/no/mixed/unknown answers
→ deterministic variable scores
→ direction, significance, veto, and confidence
```

The language model may assist research and extraction. It does not own the arithmetic or redefine the rubric during a case.

## Five-variable public layer

The first accessible layer is:

- `G` — Who bears unresolved cost, and does help restore autonomy?
- `T` — Is the causal ledger correctly targeted and proportional?
- `K` — Is evidence inspectable, challengeable, and correctable?
- `F` — Are relationships voluntary, plural, reciprocal, and escapable?
- `R` — Is actual repair and reintegration possible?

These five variables captured most of the diagnostic load in the initial historical pilot.

## Nine-variable analytical layer

The deeper layer adds:

- `M` — direction and execution of action;
- `E` — durable generative capacity;
- `S` — entropy reduction or production;
- `Q` — commitment under uncertainty and revisability.

The `C_W` consciousness/free-will wrapper remains outside the product. Public analysis cannot read hidden consciousness; it can only score observable choice architecture and response to correction.

## Historical pilot set

Initial calibration cases discussed:

1. Council of Nicaea, 325
2. Printing press, open and captured phases
3. Reformation, source-access and confessional-state phases
4. British abolition of the slave trade, 1807 onward
5. American abolition, 1865
6. Hiroshima, 1945
7. Great Society, 1964–1968
8. Mao's Cultural Revolution, 1966–1976
9. COVID emergency and prolonged-restriction phases
10. Social media, open-network and algorithmic-capture phases

The phase split is essential. One physical technology or institution may move from constructive to destructive orientation while retaining the same reach.

## Required research architecture

Future research automation should contain separate modules:

1. **Event decomposer** — converts broad questions into atomic cases.
2. **Source collector** — gathers primary records, official data, scholarship, reporting, advocate, and critic material.
3. **Claim ledger** — stores facts separately from interpretations.
4. **Evidence grader** — scores directness, source class, independence, recency, and contradiction.
5. **Rubric answerer** — answers only frozen subquestions.
6. **Adversarial panel** — advocate, critic, and arbiter passes.
7. **Scoring engine** — deterministic and API-free.
8. **Audit dashboard** — exposes every answer, source, calculation, disagreement, and refusal.

## Anti-gaming design

The strongest safeguards are:

- actor and party labels hidden on the first pass;
- identical mechanisms scored identically after identity swaps;
- unknown evidence never converted to neutral;
- facts and interpretations stored in separate fields;
- stated intentions never overwrite actual mechanisms and outcomes;
- broad people and ideologies decomposed into actions;
- one negative fact cannot be counted repeatedly without separate causal justification;
- refusal states used whenever the evidence cannot support precision.

## Known limitations

- The instrument cannot establish hidden motives or complete moral character.
- Causal attribution is often harder than outcome description.
- Ontological disputes can determine who counts as victim or offender before scoring begins.
- Weighting and thresholds require blind inter-rater calibration.
- Historical sources become less complete farther back in time.
- A reproducible score is not automatically a true score; the evidence and ontology can still be wrong.

## Immediate roadmap

### Phase 1 — Freeze the contract

- finalize every subquestion;
- prevent overlapping variables;
- define source quality weights;
- define confidence and inter-rater agreement;
- establish veto and refusal rules.

### Phase 2 — Build calibration set

- encode the initial historical cases;
- recruit independent scorers unfamiliar with the framework;
- measure agreement;
- revise ambiguous questions without changing results opportunistically.

### Phase 3 — Add research ingestion

- source adapters;
- claim extraction;
- citation ledger;
- contradiction clustering;
- evidence snapshots for reproducibility.

### Phase 4 — Build dashboard

- five-factor public view;
- full nine-factor analyst view;
- source and calculation drill-down;
- advocate/critic comparison;
- phase timeline and structural-significance visualization.

### Phase 5 — Political stress tests

Apply the frozen instrument to specific policies from opposing parties. Do not begin with global questions such as whether a president or party is good. Compare matched mechanisms and publish uncertainty alongside results.
